How it works

Briefing and trust are what make hiring expensive. Both are context problems.

So the AI does the context and coordination work — scoping, pricing, briefing, matching, verifying, settling. Humans do the part that needs taste and judgement.

1

Describe

A founder states an outcome in plain English. No brief, no spec document.

2

Scope & price

The Scoping agent turns it into work with acceptance criteria and a proposed price. The founder approves.

3

Match & brief

A doer is matched and handed exactly the context that task needs — nothing more.

4

Verify

The Review agent checks the result against the criteria written at scoping time, with reasoning.

5

Settle

On a pass, funds release. No human clicks approve. Cash or equity.

Who it's for

Two sides, one shared context layer.

For founders

A vision, not much money.

Describe the outcome. The platform turns it into scoped, priced work, finds who can do it, hands over exactly the context they need, checks the result, and settles. You never write a brief or chase a status update.

  • Plain-English goal becomes a funded task
  • Ask an agent for status instead of chasing people
  • Out of cash? Offer a share of the company instead
For skilled people

Paid for taste, not logged hours.

Work on as many companies as you like, on your own terms. No employment, no onboarding calls, no unpaid hours spent understanding a business. Over time you don't have a gig history — you have a portfolio of ownership across the companies you helped build.

  • Understand any task in two minutes, not two weeks
  • Paid on approval, never chasing an invoice
  • Take cash, or take equity in what you believe in
● Cash ● Equity ● Or a mix of both
The agents

AI that owns decisions with consequences.

Not an LLM wrapped in a form. Each agent owns one consequential decision, and a human holds the veto.

Scoping prices

Turns a goal into a spec, acceptance criteria, and what the work is worth.

Matching offers

Ranks who should do the work, with written reasoning. The founder confirms.

Briefing scopes

Hands over the context a task needs and withholds the rest — a real security boundary.

Review verifies

Judges the work against its criteria: pass, needs-changes, or fail, with reasoning.

Payout settles

Moves money the moment work passes. No human clicks approve.

Status reports

Answers "where is the auth refactor?" from task state, reviews and repo activity.

Context chat answers

Answers a doer's questions from the scoped context, so they never block on the founder.

Ingestor learns

Reads docs, repos and interviews into a queryable Company Brain.

Bring your own AI

A freelancer never has to open our app to be productive.

Point Claude, Cursor, or any MCP client at the endpoint. Your own tools read the briefs, ask questions about the company, post updates and submit work. Your editor already knows the company.

Seven blocks. That's the whole product.

Knowledge, Connections and Skills are what you give any worker — human or AI. They map exactly onto what MCP exposes: resources, tools, prompts. So the platform is an MCP server, with no translation layer.

No tool can send an invoice or move money without a human confirming it. The agent knows blocks and verbs, not screens — which is why it works everywhere.

Honest status

What works, and what doesn't.

The build started four days before a 90-day deadline, so it was never submitted to the XPRIZE. This is an honest account of where it stands.

Working today

Deployed on Cloud Run. Gemini 2.5 Pro and 2.5 Flash verified live on Vertex AI. An MCP server with enforced context scoping, both interfaces, and the seven-block model end to end as a data model.

○The loop doesn't fully close yet. No auth, state is in memory, and no reward has been settled end to end. GAPS.md lists every gap, ordered by whether the loop breaks without it.
○Not a cap table of record. Equity commitments are tracked; share issuance stays with your lawyer and company registry.
○Disputes are handled by a person, not an arbitration system.
○Nothing claims to be a model while it isn't. The two deterministic stubs are marked in-code where a Gemini call replaces them.
